Pot & Parry rules

Setup: Keep the 6 colors in their respective positions. Keep a single red between the blue & pink spots. Game starts with ball placed anywhere inside D.

No of players: The game could be played among 2-3 players. 4 players play as a team of 2 each.

Game objective: The player who reaches 50 points exactly first wins the game. The win score can be modified to 70 or 100 if needed to increase the length of the game.

Gameplay: A player on his turn can nominate any ball to pot & score points. A player is not allowed to score beyond the win score, in such cases the points remain the same as before the pot. However, certain restrictions to scoring apply based on claim rules below.
Color states: Each color except red is always in one of three states: open, claimed or exposed. All colors start as Open. Claimed or exposed state always accompany an owner along with it. Each player may at any time can only own a maximum of 3 colors claimed and exposed combined.
Open: When a player legally pots an Open color, he/she scores its value and claims it, thereby moving the color to Claimed state.
Claimed: A color is claimed by a player when he/she pots it from when it is in Open state. Claimed colors only score when the owner pots it, non-owners score 0 when potted. On reaching this maximum limit of 3 for claimed/exposed colors, a player can no longer claim any other color by potting it. When a player pots their own claimed yellow or green, it scores 4 instead of its normal value. Brown, blue, pink, and black score normally.
Exposed: A Claimed color can move to Exposed state either by a voluntary expose or a forced expose via parry. An Exposed color potted by a non-owner moves to Open after scoring (it is not claimed by that player on that pot). However, when a Exposed color is potted by the owner itself, he/she may choose to instead move it to Claimed state if doing so does not exceed his/her claim/expose limit of 3. An Exposed color can also be moved by the owner to Open if he/she legally pots red as the nominated ball.

Voluntary expose: A player may opt to move a Claimed color to Exposed at the end of their turn only if:
i) their other Claimed colors have equal or greater combined scoring value than the releasing color (yellow/green count as 4, rest of the colors count normally).
ii) they do not own another Exposed color (which may be the case either due to forced expose or a color still in Exposed after a previous voluntary expose).

Parry: A player before playing his/her stroke in a turn can call for a parry challenge by choosing a color that is either Claimed or Exposed by any other player. The challenged player must next play a parry stroke (which is an extra stroke not affecting the normal turn order stroke) with that color. If the stroke is played without any foul, play continues normally to next player in turn order. In the case of a pot, the scoring value of the color is added to the challenged player's score, he/she may opt to move the color to Claimed/Open if Exposed & can continue playing as if after a normal pot; when the stroke ends play continues to next player in turn order. However, if the challenged player fouls on the parry stroke, the challenger may score either the challenged color’s scoring value (yellow/green count as 4) or the normal foul penalty value for that stroke, whichever they prefer & add it to their score (instead of the challenged player subtracting them from his/her score). The challenged color also moves to Exposed if its Claimed under its current owner and then play continues with the challenging player taking the turn from the resulting cue-ball position. Note that any such point additions by above rules to any players may not award the points if doing so exceeds the win score. Parry challenges can only be initiated in a player turn (either at the start of the turn's stroke or after potting a ball & not in a turn gained after a parry challenge nor in a parry turn itself).

Penalties: All penalties from snooker which could be adapted to this version of the game applies normally with the only difference being penalties are subtracted from the players' scores instead. Scores are not subtracted beyond zero to keep things simple. Potting a ball when invoking a parry challenge is considered a foul & penalty points are subtracted from player's score in the way snooker penalties apply (i.e. maximum of ball on or 4).
