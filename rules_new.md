# Pot & Parry

Standard snooker rules apply unless changed below.

## Setup

- Place the six colors on their usual spots and one red midway between the blue and pink spots.
- Start with the cue ball anywhere in the D and black as the ball on.
- Respot every potted ball: colors on their usual spots and red on its starting position.

## Players

Play with two players or four players. Four may play individually or as two teams; there is no three-player game.

A team shares its score and claimed colors. Team members play in the agreed order.

- Each side may claim three colors in a two-sided game.
- Each player may claim two colors in a four-player individual game.

## Winning

The first side to reach exactly 50 wins. A pot that would take the score beyond 50 awards no points but remains legal: the turn continues and any claim still takes effect.

For a timed game, play for 30 minutes. Exactly 50 still wins immediately; otherwise, the highest score at time wins.

Ties use a blue-ball shoot-out. Reset the blue and place the cue ball anywhere in the D for each attempt. The striker must declare the pocket, and the blue may not touch a cushion except the jaws of that pocket. After equal attempts, eliminate any side below the leading shoot-out score and continue until one remains. Teammates alternate attempts.

## Claims and scoring

Red scores one and cannot be claimed. The six colors begin **open**.

- Potting an open color scores its normal value and claims it if the side is below its claim limit. Otherwise, it stays open.
- A claimed color scores only for its owner; other sides score zero for potting it.
- A claimed yellow or green scores four for its owner. Other claimed colors retain their normal values.
- A claimed color remains claimed after being potted and respotted unless lost through a parry.

Use ownership markers beside the color spots.

## Parry

A side may call one parry per normal turn, before any stroke in that turn. It may do so after a successful pot.

The challenger names an opponent's claimed color as the **challenged color**, then nominates any color for the **setup stroke**. The two colors may be the same.

The challenger must play a legal setup stroke without potting a ball. Any foul or pot cancels the parry, incurs the normal penalty and ends the challenger's turn.

After a legal setup, the owner plays the challenged color as the ball on. In a team game, the teammate next due to play takes this visit.

- **Legal stroke without a pot:** The color stays claimed and normal turn order resumes.
- **Legal pot:** The owner scores and may continue the extra visit normally. Normal turn order resumes when it ends.
- **Foul:** The owner pays the penalty, the color becomes open, and the challenger immediately resumes the interrupted turn from the resulting position.

The extra visit does not alter normal turn order. It cannot include another parry, and a challenger who resumes after a failed parry cannot call another one that turn.

## Free ball

After a foul, a free ball is awarded only if the incoming player is snookered on the red, every open color and every color claimed by their side.

The player may nominate an opponent's claimed color as the free ball and declare which scoring ball it represents. It takes that value for the stroke without changing ownership. Standard free-ball rules otherwise apply.

## Fouls

- Foul points are subtracted from the offender's score, to a minimum score of zero.
- The penalty is the highest of four, the ball on and any ball concerned, to a maximum of seven.
- The foul-and-a-miss rule is not used.
- The ball on must be declared when another player requests it.
