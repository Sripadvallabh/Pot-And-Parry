# Pot & Parry

Standard snooker rules apply unless changed below.

## Setup

- Place the six colors on their usual spots and one red midway between the blue and pink spots.
- Start with the cue ball anywhere in the D and black as the ball on.
- Respot every potted ball: colors on their usual spots and red on its starting position.

## Players

Play with two players or four players. Four may play individually or as two teams; there is no three-player game.

A **side** means one individual or one team. A team shares one score, and its members play in the agreed order.

## Basic play

Except for the opening stroke, the striker may nominate any object ball as the ball on. Colors have their normal snooker values; red scores one.

A legal pot normally allows the striker to continue the turn.

A **round** contains one scheduled normal turn for each side. Extra visits created by parries do not count. A scheduled turn belonging to a side that has already finished is skipped.

## Claims

Red cannot be claimed. Each color is either:

- **Open:** it has no owner; or
- **Claimed:** it belongs to one side.

All six colors start open. Use an ownership marker beside the spot of each claimed color. In a team game, the team owns the color jointly.

Claim limits are:

- three colors per side in a two-sided game; or
- two colors per player in a four-player individual game.

Potting an open color scores its normal value. If the side is below its limit, it also claims the color; otherwise, the color stays open.

A claimed color scores only for its owner. Other sides score zero for potting it. A claimed yellow or green scores four for its owner; other claimed colors keep their normal values.

A claimed color remains claimed after being potted and respotted unless lost through a parry.

## Winning

The target is exactly 50. When a side reaches it, that side finishes and its turn or extra visit ends immediately. Complete all remaining normal turns in the current round before deciding the winner.

- If only one side finishes during the round, it wins.
- If more than one side finishes during the round, they tie and use the blue-ball shoot-out below.

A finished side takes no more strokes and cannot be challenged.

A legal pot that would take a side beyond its target awards no points. It still continues the turn, and any claim still takes effect. A legal pot of another side's claimed color likewise scores zero but continues the turn.

### Blue-ball shoot-out

Each tied side takes one attempt with blue on its spot and the cue ball anywhere in the D. The striker declares the pocket, and the blue may not touch a cushion except the jaws of that pocket. After every tied side has taken the same number of attempts, eliminate any side below the leading shoot-out score and continue until one remains. Teammates alternate attempts.

## Parry

A side may call one parry per normal turn, before any stroke in that turn, including after a successful pot.

The challenger names another side's claimed color as the **challenged color**, then nominates any color for the **setup stroke**. The two may be the same color.

The challenger must play a legal setup stroke without potting a ball. A foul or pot cancels the parry, incurs the normal penalty and ends the challenger's turn.

After a legal setup, the owner plays the challenged color as the ball on. In a team game, the teammate next due to play takes this extra visit.

- **Legal stroke without a pot:** The color stays claimed and normal turn order resumes.
- **Legal pot:** The owner scores and may continue the extra visit normally. The parry is then defended; any later foul is an ordinary foul. Normal turn order resumes when the visit ends.
- **Foul, including failing to hit the challenged color:** The owner pays the penalty, the color becomes open, and the challenger immediately resumes the interrupted turn from the resulting position.

The extra visit does not alter normal turn order. It cannot include another parry, and the challenger cannot call another one after resuming the interrupted turn.

## Timed showdown

For a timed game, complete the current round when 30 minutes expires. If nobody finishes on 50, each side declares one personal target based on its score at the end of that round:

- the next higher multiple of five; or
- the next higher multiple of ten.

For example, a side on 33 may choose 35 or 40. A side already on a multiple must choose a strictly higher target: a side on 30 may choose 35 or 40.

Each target remains fixed. Begin a new round from the existing table position and continue in normal turn order. The usual exact-score and round-completion rules apply separately to each side's target, so multiple sides that finish during the same round tie. Foul penalties do not change a declared target.

## Free ball

After a foul, a free ball is awarded only if the incoming player is snookered on every ball they can score from: red, every open color and every color claimed by their side.

The player may nominate another side's claimed color as the free ball and declare which scoring ball it represents. It takes that value for the stroke without changing ownership. Standard free-ball rules otherwise apply.

## Fouls

- Foul points are subtracted from the offender's score, to a minimum score of zero.
- The penalty is the highest of four, the ball on and any ball concerned, to a maximum of seven.
- The foul-and-a-miss rule is not used.
- The ball on must be declared when another player requests it.
